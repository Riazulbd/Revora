# Revora — QA audit, September 2026

Audit run: 2026-09-16. Author: automated audit, Phase A.
Subject: commit `f2e7532`, app version 2.6 (versionCode 17).

This document is the whole record of the audit. It states what was tested, how,
what came back, what is broken, what is undecided, and what was never checked at
all. It is written for an engineer who was not in the room: every finding names a
file and a line, and every claim that rests on a judgement call says so.

**Read section 1 before anything else.** A large part of this app was not tested,
and the report is only useful if you know which part.

---

## 1. Verdict

### Not ready.

Not ready to ship, on two separate grounds.

**Ground one — defects found.** One critical and seven high-severity defects were
confirmed by reproduction, and 40 of 239 checks failed:

- **Critical.** `calculateOwnershipCost` adds minor units across different
  currencies and labels the result with the bike's current currency. A rider who
  logs in BDT, switches the bike to USD in Settings and logs one more fill sees a
  running-cost headline that is the arithmetic sum of poisha and cents — a number
  in no currency at all (`MON-10`, `src/calculations/expenses.ts:209`).
- **High.** The storage boundary caps every money amount at two decimals, so the
  seven ISO 3-decimal currencies (BHD, IQD, JOD, KWD, LYD, OMR, TND) lose their
  thousandths on every save. 0.305 KWD/L is read back as 0.310 (`MON-09-8`,
  `src/repositories/wire.ts:53`). This violates non-negotiable 4 on the write
  path.
- **High.** The total the rider typed on the receipt is not what gets stored: the
  save path re-derives it from a rounded price per litre, so BDT 1,000.00 over
  7 L persists as BDT 1,000.02 (`MON-02b`, `src/calculations/fuel.ts:104`).
- **High.** Per-litre prices ignore the currency exponent, so every 0-decimal
  currency shows a price 100x too small ('JPY 1.50' for ¥150/L) and 3-decimal
  prices collapse to 'KWD 0' (`MON-03b-1/2/4`, `src/utils/format.ts:142`,
  `src/calculations/money.ts:91`). Display only, on five surfaces.
- **High.** The Pro assistant is handed a cost-per-distance figure ten times
  smaller than the one the Dashboard shows beside it (`AST-14`,
  `src/features/assistant/buildContext.ts:56`).
- **High.** Fast Capture is a dead end: `useCompleteCapture` has zero call sites,
  so no queued capture can ever be completed, and the delete path is gated on the
  `completed` status that nothing sets. A Quick note can never be opened,
  completed or removed (`OFF-20`, `OFF-21`, `src/app/capture.tsx:86,226`).
- **High.** Every success toast, every `router.back()` and the sync tracker are
  wired to the Firestore promise, which the Android SDK resolves only on server
  acknowledgement. Offline, the Save spinner does not clear, the screen does not
  pop and 'Saved to offline queue' never appears — for an app whose headline
  promise is logging a fill at the pump with no signal (`OFF-23`, `OFF-25`,
  `src/features/shared/useRecordMutation.ts:41`). **This one is code-inspection
  only and must be confirmed on a device (check ND-3) before it is fixed or
  dismissed.**
- **High.** Linked multi-document saves are sequential awaits with no batch. Kill
  the app offline after saving a service and the service exists while its
  maintenance-rule resets and item rows never do (`OFF-24`,
  `src/features/services/useServiceMutations.ts:100`).

**Ground two — most of the product was never exercised.** All 239 checks below
are static: unit tests against the calculation layer, code inspection, and
emulator runs of the security rules and the billing domain. Not one of them ran
the app.

### What was NOT verified

Say this plainly: **the app was not run**. Nothing in this report proves that any
screen renders, that any button works, or that a record can be saved from the
user interface.

- **No device journeys.** Twelve Maestro flows covering the core fixtures were
  written (`e2e/audit/*.yaml`) and **none were executed** — no device or emulator
  was attached to this run (`PB-00` … `PB-70`, all NOT TESTED). A further **69
  device-only checks** are enumerated in Appendix A and none were run. That
  includes every keyboard-overlap check, every TalkBack pass, every dark-mode
  contrast measurement, every large-text screenshot, and every offline
  kill-and-relaunch.
- **No real Google Play billing.** `PLAY-01` is **BLOCKED**: purchase, renewal,
  refund, revocation, grace period, account hold and pending payment were not
  tested against the live Play Billing Library or Developer API, because no
  licence-tester session was available. `npm run test:billing` replaces
  `play.get` and `play.acknowledge` with in-memory maps
  (`functions/test/billing.integration.cjs:36-46`), so it proves the grant rule
  and the replay guard — **not** Play integration, service-account linkage, RTDN
  topic wiring, or acknowledgement against the real API.
- **No visual comparison against the redesign.** `design/redesign/*.dc.html` is
  the visual source of truth per CLAUDE.md, and no artboard-versus-screen
  comparison was performed. Block order, spacing, radii, type roles and the
  designed light/dark/empty/error/offline/keyboard states are all unverified.
- **No native build.** No APK or AAB was produced, so nothing here says the app
  compiles for release, and `android/` was not regenerated.
- **No performance, battery, memory or cold-start measurement.** No crash
  testing, no low-memory behaviour, no large-dataset behaviour (the biggest
  fixture in this audit is a handful of records).
- **No multi-device, multi-account or migration testing** beyond what the
  security rules prove. The two-device conflict policy is undefined *and*
  untested (`OFF-27`, `GAP-09`).
- **No web product.** Revora shares its Firestore schema with a web product that
  was not available to this audit. Several findings (archived bikes, `drained`
  and `theft` adjustment reasons, `file://` photo paths) turn on what the web
  side does, and that was inferred from the schema, not observed.
- **Accessibility was audited statically only.** Touch-target sizes and
  accessible names were measured by rendering components in Jest. No screen
  reader was ever run.

### One more caveat, and it matters

The repository was being edited by eleven other agents while this audit ran. The
evidence in section 3 was captured against commit `f2e7532` with a dirty working
tree. Re-running the suites at the time of writing, **five accessibility findings
no longer reproduce** — `A11Y-03`, `A11Y-04`, `A11Y-05`, `A11Y-06` and `A11Y-15`
are fixed in the working tree by the component work in progress (`Toggle` now
uses `HIT_SIZE`, `Button` now sets `accessibilityLabel={label}` so a busy Save
keeps its name). Those rows are left in the matrix as recorded, with the
correction noted in section 4. Treat every other finding as valid for `f2e7532`
and re-run before acting on it.

### Where a FAIL was itself wrong

`MON-04b` is recorded as FAIL and **it should not be**. The auditor expected the
early cost-per-km caveat to name the 900 km remaining to the settling point. The
shipped copy — "Based on 100 km so far, so one large purchase still moves it a
lot. It settles around 1,000 km." — names the tracked distance and the threshold
instead. Three independent verifiers agreed that the 900 km figure exists only in
the audit brief: `src/utils/format.test.ts:88-104` and
`e2e/audit/20-costs-fixture.yaml:14,112` both specify the shipped wording, commit
`aed6eb6` deliberately introduced it, and the design files contain no caveat copy
at all. **Correct reading: PASS.** The brief's fixture is what needs updating,
not the code. The auditor's own severity of `none` conceded as much.

Two severities were also disputed on re-verification, in opposite directions, and
both are recorded in section 4: `MON-02b` (high, argued down to medium on
magnitude) and `MON-03b-4` (low, argued up to medium because it is reachable and
not latent).

### Limitations of this environment

1. **Nothing was observed through the UI.** Every "observed" value in section 3
   came from a function return, a rendered component tree in Jest, or reading the
   source. Where a finding depends on runtime behaviour it says so and names a
   device check.
2. **The Android Firestore SDK was not exercised.** `OFF-23`'s claim — that the
   `set()` promise does not resolve offline — is read from the native Java source
   in `node_modules` and from documented SDK semantics. It is the single most
   consequential finding in the offline section and it is unproven. Check ND-3
   decides it.
3. **The emulator-gated audit suites do not run under `npm test`.** They skip
   themselves when `FIRESTORE_EMULATOR_HOST` is absent, and `jest.rules.config.js`
   matches only `tests/**`, so `npm run test:rules` does not pick them up either.
   Section 8 gives the exact commands.
4. **`functions/lib/domain.js` must be built** before the billing audit suite can
   run; it reads the compiled module, not the TypeScript source.
5. **Component tests are on unofficial tooling.** `@testing-library/react-native`
   14.0.1 is declared but its peer `test-renderer` is not installed, so the
   accessibility suite falls back to `react-test-renderer` picked up transitively
   (`A11Y-27`). This is still true in the working tree.
6. **Single locale, single timezone, single unit system for most fixtures.**
   Everything was measured in English with BDT and kilometres unless a test
   explicitly says otherwise. Multi-currency behaviour was probed only where
   noted.
7. **The interaction inventory is derived from source, not from a running app.**
   `e2e/tools/inventory.mjs` walks `src/app` and `src/features` and reports what
   is rendered, not what is reachable. It found 46 screens carrying a screen id,
   266 controls, 97 input fields and 347 addressable testIDs — and **33 controls
   with no stable testID**, which cannot be driven by a test or named to a screen
   reader. Ten of those are listed in Appendix A.9 as the ones the Phase B flows
   have to reach by visible text instead.

## 3. Coverage matrix

239 checks across 8 areas: **166 PASS, 40 FAIL, 32 NOT TESTED, 1 BLOCKED.**

By method: 150 automated unit tests, 52 code inspections, 19 emulator rules
checks, 6 emulator billing checks, 12 written-but-not-run device flows.

Three things to know before reading the tables.

- **A PASS means the observed behaviour matched the stated expectation**, not
  that the behaviour is desirable. Several rows pass while recording a design
  problem; those carry a severity and reappear in section 4 (`MIL-07e`,
  `MIL-09f`).
- **A FAIL does not always mean a red test.** Where the defect is in code the
  audit could not change, the test asserts the *observed* (wrong) behaviour so it
  stays green and pins the bug; the row says so. Re-running the audit suites
  today, **12 assertions are actually red**, all in `money.audit.test.ts` (11) and
  `specgaps-assistant.audit.test.ts` (1).
- **NOT TESTED means exactly that** — usually because the check needs a device, a
  licence tester, or a product decision that does not exist yet.

### Critical

#### D1 — Ownership cost sums minor units across currencies

- **Rows:** `MON-10` (critical), `GAP-07` (low, same root cause seen from the ledger side)
- **Where:** `src/calculations/expenses.ts:209` (`totalMinor` / `breakdown`), consumed by `src/calculations/ownership.ts:69` and `src/calculations/analytics.ts:165-183`
- **Reproduction:** build a ledger with one BDT line of 100,000 minor (BDT 1,000.00) and one USD line of 5,000 minor ($50.00). Call `calculateOwnershipCost`. In the app: log records in BDT, then Settings → Currency → USD, then log one more fill.
- **Expected:** a guard, a refusal, or per-currency subtotals. Anything except one number.
- **Actual:** `runningCostMinor = 105_000`, `totalMinor = 105_000`, `breakdown.total = 105_000`, labelled with the bike's *current* currency. `buildLedger` does stamp each line with its own record's currency — that part is correct — and then every aggregator ignores the field.
- **Impact:** the Dashboard running-cost card, the Costs tab total, cost per km, the monthly chart and the assistant's answer all become a meaningless number after a currency change, with nothing on screen indicating it. Reachable by an ordinary Settings action: `src/features/settings/useUnitsSettings.tsx:59-61` patches only the bike ("relabelled, never converted") while existing records keep their own currency.
- **Note:** `GAP-07`'s verifier observed that the parsers currently stamp every record with the bike's current currency at read time (`src/repositories/parsers.ts:197-205`), which would mask the mixed ledger for app-written data. That is a coincidence of the read path, not a guard — and it is exactly why the figure is wrong rather than obviously broken: the amounts keep their old magnitudes and get a new label.

### High

#### D2 — The storage boundary truncates 3-decimal currencies

- **Rows:** `MON-09-8` (high)
- **Where:** `src/repositories/wire.ts:53` — `divideRound(minor * 100, scale) / 100`
- **Reproduction:** `minorToMajor(1_305, 'KWD')` → 1.31 (expected 1.305); round-trip back → 1,310. Same for a 305-fils price → 310.
- **Expected:** the stored major-unit amount round-trips exactly at the currency's own exponent.
- **Actual:** every money field is capped at two decimals on the write path — fuel price and total, service labour/parts/total/quote, part cost, accessory cost, expense amount, purchase price, allowance (`src/repositories/serialisers.ts:45-273`).
- **Impact:** for BHD, IQD, JOD, KWD, LYD, OMR and TND, every amount is silently rounded to the nearest 10 minor units on save. It violates non-negotiable 4 on the money path, and because it happens at the wire boundary the data is wrong in Firestore, not just on screen. The shared web product reads the same rounded values.

#### D3 — The stored total is re-derived from a rounded price, not the receipt

- **Rows:** `MON-02b` (high; two independent verifiers argued it down to **medium** on magnitude — see below)
- **Where:** `src/calculations/fuel.ts:104-110` (`normaliseFuelAmounts` prefers `pricePerLitreMinor` whenever it is > 0), fed by `src/features/fuel/useFuelMutations.ts:60`
- **Reproduction:** Add fuel with the price box pre-filled from the last fill. Type 7 litres, then overwrite Total with 1000. `recalculate('total')` writes the derived price 142.86 back into the price box (`src/app/fuel/add.tsx:183-184`). Submit re-parses both boxes and passes price 14,286 *and* total 100,000 into the draft.
- **Expected:** `totalCostMinor` stays 100,000 (BDT 1,000.00 — what the rider typed and what the form displayed).
- **Actual:** 100,002. `7_000 × 14_286 / 1_000 = 100_002`. The same drift appears for 3 L (99,999), 6 L (100,002) and 9 L (99,999) — any litres that do not divide the total evenly, which is the common case.
- **Impact:** the stored record and every downstream sum disagree with the receipt and with what the form showed. Hidden on the 0-decimal fuel list, visible in the edit form, the CSV export (`src/features/premium/exportCsv.ts:23-26`), the proof pack, and accumulating in spend totals. For a 0-decimal currency the drift is a whole yen per fill.
- **Root cause, stated precisely:** the form tracks which box the rider last edited, but that ref never leaves the component, so the save path cannot tell an entered price from a derived one. The existing test `fuel.test.ts:113` documents a "price wins" rule that assumed the price was entered.
- **Severity dispute:** the auditor recorded high. Two verifiers reduced it to medium because the error is bounded to roughly `litresMl / 2000` minor units per fill. Both agreed it is real, systematic and reachable. It is kept under High here because it is a *stored* money value that contradicts the rider's receipt; the counter-argument is on the record.

#### D4 — Per-litre prices ignore the currency exponent

- **Rows:** `MON-03b-1` (high, 0-dp currencies), `MON-03b-2` (medium, 3-dp currencies), `MON-03b-4` (low as recorded; three verifiers raised it to **medium** because it is reachable, not latent)
- **Where:** `src/utils/format.ts:142` — `formatMoney(v, currency, { decimals: v % 100 === 0 ? 0 : 2 })` — and the branch it lands in, `src/calculations/money.ts:91`
- **Reproduction:** `formatPricePerVolume(150, 'JPY')` → `'JPY 1.50'` (expected `'JPY 150'`). `formatPricePerVolume(300, 'KWD')` → `'KWD 0'` (expected `'KWD 0.300'`). `formatMoney(1_305, 'JPY', { decimals: 2 })` → `'JPY 13.05'` (expected `'JPY 1,305.00'`).
- **Expected:** the decimals come from `currencyDecimals(currency)`, as the adjacent `formatRate` already does at `format.ts:131`.
- **Actual:** the `v % 100` heuristic hard-codes a 2-decimal exponent. For a 0-exponent currency `decimals(2) >= scale(0)` leaves the value unscaled and then divides by 100 — the point moves two places. For a 3-exponent currency, a value that is a multiple of 100 selects 0 decimals and `divideRound(300, 1000)` gives 0.
- **Impact:** verified probe results — 100 fils/L → `'KWD 0'`, 85 fils → `'KWD 0.09'`, 165 JPY → `'JPY 1.65'`, 1,650 KRW → `'KRW 16.50'`. Every real Kuwaiti pump price is either zeroed or misrounded; every 0-exponent currency is 100x low. Five rider-facing surfaces: `src/app/(tabs)/fuel.tsx:139`, `src/features/fuel/FuelRow.tsx:48-52`, `src/app/fuel/[id].tsx:129`, `src/app/analytics.tsx:404`, `src/features/assistant/answerQuestion.ts:141`. BDT — the home market — is unaffected, which is why this survived.
- **Stored data is correct.** This is display only. It still breaks non-negotiable 6 in spirit: a non-zero price rendering as `KWD 0` is a wrong figure shown with no indication.

#### D5 — The Pro assistant is told a cost per km ten times too small

- **Rows:** `AST-14` (high)
- **Where:** `src/features/assistant/buildContext.ts:56-57`
- **Reproduction:** `buildAssistantContext` over a fixture whose `costPerDistanceScaled` is 101,250. `RATE_SCALE` is 100 (`src/calculations/units.ts:147`), so that is 1,012.5 minor per km → `'BDT 10 per km'`.
- **Expected:** `'BDT 10 per km'` — the same figure the Dashboard renders beside it.
- **Actual:** `'BDT 1 per km'`. The context divides the scaled figure by 1,000 instead of by `RATE_SCALE`.
- **Impact:** the model is briefed with a number that contradicts the screen the rider is looking at, which is the exact failure the shared-calculation rule (non-negotiable 1) exists to prevent. The existing `buildContext.test.ts` uses a fixture of 4,830,000 and never asserts the produced string, so it did not catch this.

#### D6 — "Saved" means acknowledged by the server, not accepted locally

- **Rows:** `OFF-23` (high), `OFF-25` (low, the sync indicator half)
- **Where:** `src/features/shared/useRecordMutation.ts:41-52` — toast, `trackWrite`, and `onSuccess` (`router.back()`, sheet close) all run only in `onSuccess`
- **Reproduction (code):** `documentSet` chains the Android SDK Task from `DocumentReference.set()` (`node_modules/@react-native-firebase/firestore/android/src/reactnative/java/io/invertase/firebase/firestore/NativeRNFBTurboFirestoreDocument.java:185-207`) and resolves the JS promise only on that Task's completion, which the Firebase SDK completes on backend acknowledgement.
- **Expected:** the write is accepted locally, so the button releases, the toast shows and the screen pops immediately — offline included.
- **Actual (predicted):** offline, the Save spinner persists (`src/components/Button.tsx:60-64`), the Quick-note sheet stays open (`src/app/capture.tsx:210-221`), `'Saved to offline queue'` never appears, and the fuel detail delete does not navigate back. `trackWrite()` fires after the server already acknowledged, so `waitForPendingWrites` resolves immediately, `'pending'`/`'failed'` are at most a flicker and `'Retrying'` (`src/app/settings.tsx:109`) is unreachable.
- **What still works:** the data *is* queued durably and appears in lists via the listener, and the `'Waiting for connection'` label is driven by NetInfo, so that one stays truthful.
- **Impact:** this is the app's headline promise — log a fill at the pump with no signal — and as read, the rider gets a hung button and no confirmation.
- **UNPROVEN.** This is a code-inspection finding about native SDK behaviour. **Run device check ND-3 before doing anything about it.** If this build's SDK resolves locally, the severity drops to none.

#### D7 — Linked multi-document saves are not atomic

- **Rows:** `OFF-24` (high)
- **Where:** `src/features/services/useServiceMutations.ts:100-146`, `src/features/fuel/useFuelMutations.ts:96-125`. `writeBatch` appears once in the whole of `src/` and only in `deleteWhereBike` (`src/repositories/base.ts:282`).
- **Reproduction:** offline, save a service with a ticked maintenance rule and one item line, then force-stop the app before reconnecting. (Device check ND-6.)
- **Expected:** one batch, or an order that survives the process dying.
- **Actual:** sequential awaits — save the service, patch each rule, delete/create items, sync the bike odometer. Combined with D6, the first await blocks until reconnect, so steps 2..N run only if the JS process is still alive.
- **Impact:** a service saved without its rule resets or item rows; a fill saved without its adjustments and with a stale odometer for the web product. Firestore flushes its queue FIFO, so ordering within what *was* written is safe — the problem is what was never enqueued.

#### D8 — Fast Capture is a queue with no exit

- **Rows:** `OFF-20` (high), `OFF-21` (high)
- **Where:** `src/app/capture.tsx:86-99` and `:226`; `src/features/capture/useCaptureMutations.ts:50`
- **Reproduction:** queue a Quick note and a Receipt photo, then tap each. (Device check ND-12.)

## 5. Calculation audit

Every expected value below was computed by hand from the fixture, written into
the test as a literal, and only then compared against the code. Nothing in this
section asked the module under test to produce its own expectation.

Units throughout: distance in integer metres, volume in integer millilitres,
money in integer minor units, dates as `'YYYY-MM-DD'` strings.

### 5.1 The core fuel fixture — A, B, C

| Entry | Odometer | Fill type | Volume | Price/L | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 4,845 km = 4,845,000 m | Full | 9 L = 9,000 ml | 14,500 | 130,500 |
| B | 4,965 km = 4,965,000 m | Partial | 3 L = 3,000 ml | 14,500 | 43,500 |
| C | 5,118 km = 5,118,000 m | Full | 4 L = 4,000 ml | 14,500 | 58,000 |

**Hand computation.** A Full fill opens the interval and a Full fill closes it, so
A→C is the one and only interval; B sits inside it.

- Distance: 5,118,000 − 4,845,000 = **273,000 m** (273 km)
- Fuel: 3,000 + 4,000 = **7,000 ml** (7 L). A's 9,000 ml is *excluded* — it filled
  the tank at the start of the stretch, it did not power it.
- Efficiency: 273,000 / 7,000 = **39 exactly** m/ml = **39.0 km/L**
- `partialFills` 1, `unknownFills` 0, `adjustmentMl` 0, `rawFuelMl` 7,000
- Confidence: **medium**, because a partial sat inside the interval
- Summary: count 1, `latest` = `average` = 39, `provisional` null, `blockedBy`
  null, and `best`/`worst`/`lastFive` withheld below 5 intervals

**Observed.** Exactly that. `metresPerMl === 39`, `formatMileageValue` → `'39.0'`,
confidence `'medium'`, summary as stated. Rows `MIL-01a`, `MIL-01b`, `MIL-01c` —
**PASS**.

**Edit B from 3 L to 3.5 L.**

- Fuel: 3,500 + 4,000 = **7,500 ml**
- Efficiency: 273,000 / 7,500 = **36.4 exactly** → `'36.4'`

**Observed.** `fuelMl` 7,500, `metresPerMl === 36.4`, display `'36.4'`. Row
`MIL-02` — **PASS**. The figure is recomputed from the records on read; nothing
stale survives the edit, which is non-negotiable 3 working as intended.

### 5.2 The money triad — 9 L at BDT 145

**Hand computation**, in integer minor units with BDT's exponent of 2:

- Total from litres and price: `9,000 ml × 14,500 minor/L ÷ 1,000 ml/L` =
  130,500,000 / 1,000 = **130,500 minor = BDT 1,305.00**
- Price from total and litres: `130,500 × 1,000 / 9,000` = **14,500**
- Litres from total and price: `130,500 × 1,000 / 14,500` = **9,000 ml**

**Observed.** 130,500; 14,500; 9,000. Row `MON-01` — **PASS**. The triad is exact
and reversible, with no float anywhere in the path.

**Two caveats on the same fixture:**

1. The third leg is derived correctly by `deriveFuelAmounts` and then discarded
   by the form, which never calls `setLitres` — defect D11 (`PB-11-FORM`).
2. When the division is *not* exact, the stored total is re-derived from the
   rounded price rather than kept as typed — defect D3 (`MON-02b`). The rounding
   rule itself was probed and is **half away from zero**: 5/2 → 3, 7/2 → 4,
   −5/2 → −3, 1/3 → 0, 2/3 → 1 (`MON-02`, PASS; documented at
   `src/calculations/money.ts:62`).

### 5.3 The cost fixture — 100 km, BDT 1,000 eligible, BDT 500 accessory

Fixture in minor units: fuel 60,000 + service 40,000 + accessory 50,000, over
100 km of tracked distance (100,000 m).

**Hand computation.**

- Total spent: 60,000 + 40,000 + 50,000 = **150,000 minor = BDT 1,500.00**
- Eligible for cost per distance: 60,000 + 40,000 = **100,000 minor = BDT 1,000.00**.

- Cost per km: 100,000 minor / 100 km = **1,000 minor/km = BDT 10.00 per km**

- Settled threshold is 1,000,000 m, so at 100,000 m the figure is **not settled**
  and carries its caveat
- Breakdown: fuel 60,000; services 40,000; parts 0; accessories 50,000; other 0;
  total 150,000

**Observed.** All six assertions as expected. Row `MON-04` — **PASS**.

**Related checks on the same figure:**

- `MON-05` — the opening odometer is excluded from tracked distance:
  4,945,000 − 4,845,000 = 100,000 m, not 4,945,000. **PASS.**
- `MON-06` — zero or negative distance gives `null`, never 0, and renders `'—'`.
  **PASS**, and this is non-negotiable 6 holding.
- `MON-07` — purchase price enters the headline and cost/km only under
  `costBasis: 'total'`: 28,500,000 + 150,000 = 28,650,000; per km

  the note that `costBasis` still echoes `'total'` when it silently fell back to
  running figures (gap G20).
- `MON-04b` — the caveat wording. Recorded FAIL; **the FAIL is wrong**, see
  section 1.
- `MON-04c` — the same figure in miles: 1,000 minor/km × 1.609344 = 1,609.344

### 5.4 What else the calculation layer got right

Worth recording, because it is the part of the app that holds up:

- Only a Full fill closes an interval; partial and unknown fills never do
  (`MIL-03a/b/c`). Consecutive Full fills produce one interval each (`MIL-03d`).
- Same-day entries are ordered by odometer regardless of array or creation order
  (`MIL-03e`).
- Every division guards both denominators, and zero distance, zero fuel and
  negative net fuel all produce no interval and no `NaN`/`Infinity` anywhere in
  the summary (`MIL-03f`, `MIL-04a/b/c/d`).
- The average is distance-weighted — total distance over total fuel, not a mean
  of ratios: 100 km/2 L and 300 km/10 L give 400,000/12,000 = 33.3, explicitly
  not 40 (`MIL-10a`).
- `adjustmentMlInRange` is half-open on the left and closed on the right, so an
  adjustment dated on the opening fill belongs to the previous interval and one
  dated on the closing fill belongs to this one (`MIL-09d`, `MIL-09e`) — except
  when the two dates are the same, which is defect D12.
- All seven adjustment reasons net identically and signed; several adjustments in
  one interval sum (`MIL-09a/b/c`); deleting one restores the original figure
  (`MIL-09h`).
- Mileage renders to exactly one decimal in every probe, including the L/100km
  inversion (`MIL-11`).
- Maintenance boundaries are exact at every edge: −1 m is overdue, 0 m is due
  soon, `warnAhead` is due soon, `warnAhead + 1` is upcoming, `2 × warnAhead` is
  upcoming, `2 × warnAhead + 1` is good (`MNT-01` … `MNT-06`).
- Dashboard and Analytics read the same `analyseBike()` mileage fields; no screen
  calls `calculateMileage*` directly (`MIL-12`). `exportCsv.ts:38` calls
  `calculateMileageIntervals` directly — same function, same inputs, no drift,
  but it bypasses `analyseBike`.
